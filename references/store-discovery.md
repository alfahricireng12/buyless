# Dynamic store discovery

Do not use a bundled retailer list, marketplace allowlist or country registry. Discover relevant sources afresh from the user's product, city and country. Store names in actual results must come from current research, not a fixed catalog.

## Start from the destination

Build queries using the exact model or category, locality, country and locally used commerce language. Use available maps/place search for nearby outlets, ordinary web search for retailers and marketplaces, and manufacturer sources for distribution channels. Without a maps tool, use branch pages and public directories and disclose distance limitations.

Adapt these patterns, translating commerce terms while preserving model identifiers:

```text
[model] [variant] [city] [country]
[product category] shop [district] [city]
[model] buy price [country]
[model] pickup in stock [city]
[brand] authorized dealer [country]
[model] marketplace [country]
[model] shipping to [destination country]
```

Search local scripts and common transliterations where useful. Country-specific domains are clues, not proof of seller location, legitimacy or shipping coverage. Do not limit discovery to stores appearing on the first results page. Follow relevant independent retailers, marketplace sellers and outlet links as new leads emerge.

## Expand outward

1. Discover nearby physical shops and branches that plausibly sell the requested category. Verify the actual pickup outlet and product inventory separately.
2. Expand to retailers and marketplaces across the user's country that deliver to the city. Include independent shops and specialist sellers, not only highly ranked platforms.
3. Discover overseas sellers that can serve the destination when consistent with the user's constraints. Keep unknown shipping and import costs visible.
4. Once candidates are known, search their official promotion pages and brand/platform campaigns. Do not maintain a static coupon list.

A strict radius constrains nearby recommendations, not the meaning of nationwide delivery. Do not call a distant shipping warehouse a nearby store. Expand a pickup radius only when consistent with the user's instructions and label wider alternatives.

## Establish actual coverage

A map entry is a lead until inventory and price are observed. A marketplace is a discovery channel, not evidence that every seller on it was searched. A branch's stock is not another branch's stock. Record blocked sources and unpriced leads; never count them as verified offers.

Consolidate the same seller/listing/variant, resolve marketplace identities, and trace mirror pages back to their original source. Follow the rounds, source-family ledger and stopping rules in [search-protocol.md](search-protocol.md). Report the stores actually found and checked, remaining gaps and stop reason. No bounded web search can prove that all physical and online shops have been searched.
