# Location and fulfillment

Location is required for recommendations. Accept a locality and country where necessary, postcode, user-selected point, or explicitly shared device location. Use the least precision needed. A city supports discovery; it may not support an exact delivery quote or distance from the user.

If location is missing, ask only where to search. If it is already in the request or conversation, do not ask again. “Jakarta” can be a destination and nearby search area; it does not mean every eligible seller must be based in Jakarta.

For nearby discovery without a user radius, use a visibly stated initial area, such as roughly 10 km around a named locality reference point. Treat it as approximate until the reference point and distances are checked. Label expansions; do not violate an explicit radius limit.

Separate:

- Physical outlet and verified pickup availability.
- Seller business address.
- Warehouse or dispatch location.
- Destination delivery eligibility.

A store's directory page proves neither product stock nor price. A national chain's online price may not apply to a particular branch. List unconfirmed shops as leads, with contact/source links where available; do not contact them automatically.

Show whether distance is road distance, straight-line distance, or a locality approximation. If unknown, say so. Never invent coordinates or a distance to make the table look complete.

For international listings, verify the actual destination, seller exclusions, regional product compatibility, warranty territory, delivery cost, and import uncertainty. Freight forwarding requires a separate user-selected assumption; it is not automatic shipping eligibility.

For pickup, show item payment and travel cost separately. Without a travel-cost estimate supplied or explicitly accepted by the user, do not declare pickup cheaper than a fully delivered offer solely from the sticker price.
